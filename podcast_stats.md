# Denmark Migration: Podcast Talking Points
*Data: Statistics Denmark (Statbank VAN1AAR / VAN2AAR), 2020–2025*

---

## 1. The framing is almost certainly cherry-picking 2022 as a baseline

The single biggest thing distorting migration narratives right now: **2022 was a historic outlier** driven by Ukrainian displacement. Net migration hit **+58,256** — roughly double any other year in this dataset. Any year-on-year decline measured from that peak is largely mechanical regression to the mean, not a signal about Denmark's attractiveness.

| Year | Immigration | Emigration | Net |
|------|-------------|------------|-----|
| 2020 | 70,340 | 59,420 | **+10,920** |
| 2021 | 76,278 | 49,803 | **+26,475** |
| 2022 | 121,183 | 62,927 | **+58,256** ← Ukraine spike |
| 2023 | 98,344 | 68,172 | **+30,172** |
| 2024 | 101,724 | 69,467 | **+32,257** |
| 2025 | 102,031 | 70,340 | **+31,691** |

**2023–2025 net migration has been remarkably stable at ~31k/year.** That's not a crumbling brand — that's a plateau.

---

## 2. "Decrease in certain groups is statistical noise" — what the numbers actually say

Year-on-year change in **non-Danish net migration**:

| Period | Change in net |
|--------|--------------|
| 2020→2021 | +19,405 |
| 2021→2022 | +34,349 |
| 2022→2023 | −29,928 |
| 2023→2024 | **+1,488** |
| 2024→2025 | **−221** |

Standard deviation of non-Danish net migration across the 6 years: **±15,724**

The moves in 2023→2024 (+1,488) and 2024→2025 (−221) are **well within one standard deviation** — they are indistinguishable from noise in any honest statistical framing. Anyone citing those as evidence of a trend is doing something rhetorically rather than analytically.

## 3. Are more internationals leaving than arriving?

**No — not even close.** But the ratio is worth watching.

| Year | Intl. in | Intl. out | Ratio (out/in) |
|------|----------|-----------|----------------|
| 2020 | 48,352 | 43,368 | 0.90 |
| 2021 | 57,659 | 33,270 | 0.58 |
| 2022 | 102,648 | 43,910 | 0.43 |
| 2023 | 79,252 | 50,442 | 0.64 |
| 2024 | 82,806 | 52,508 | 0.63 |
| 2025 | 83,990 | 53,913 | 0.64 |

The ratio stabilising around **0.63–0.64** means roughly **2 people arrive for every 1.3 that leave**. Denmark is not in outflow territory. The concern, if any, is that this ratio *was* 0.43 in 2022 and has normalised upward — but again, 2022 was a refugee-driven anomaly.

---

## 4. The story that IS real: EU citizens quietly reversing

This is the one number that deserves genuine scrutiny — not as a brand collapse, but as a structural shift worth watching.

| Year | EU in | EU out | EU net |
|------|-------|--------|--------|
| 2020 | 49,112 | 38,567 | **+10,545** |
| 2021 | 55,271 | 34,503 | **+20,768** |
| 2022 | 59,427 | 40,606 | **+18,821** |
| 2023 | 55,763 | 43,018 | **+12,745** |
| 2024 | 54,146 | 44,427 | **+9,719** |
| 2025 | 52,158 | 43,342 | **+8,816** |

EU **immigration is down ~12%** from its 2022 peak. EU **emigration is up ~13%** from 2020. Net EU migration has more than halved since 2021. This is real and structural — and it predates any specific news cycle. The honest framing: **EU labour mobility is tightening across the board** as post-Brexit/post-COVID patterns normalise and competing destinations (Germany, Netherlands, Ireland) have also sharpened their offers.

---

## 5. G7 and Global North: small numbers, stable signal

G7 net migration (the high-skilled knowledge-worker proxy):

| Year | G7 net |
|------|--------|
| 2020 | +1,362 |
| 2021 | +5,037 |
| 2022 | +7,085 |
| 2023 | +4,809 |
| 2024 | +4,650 |
| 2025 | +4,025 |

The decline from 2022 to 2025 looks steep in percentage terms (~43%) but the **absolute numbers are small** — we're talking about a difference of ~3,000 people across all 7 countries over 3 years. That's not a signal that can be meaningfully attributed to reputational damage from specific stories. It's within the margin of visa policy changes, bilateral agreements, and normal mobility fluctuations.

## 6. Who IS driving emigration right now?

Top citizenship groups by emigration volume, 2023–2025 combined:

| Citizenship | Emigrants |
|-------------|-----------|
| Denmark (returning nationals) | 50,847 |
| Romania | 14,841 |
| Ukraine | 14,170 |
| USA | 12,172 |
| Poland | 10,290 |
| Germany | 8,882 |
| Italy | 7,296 |
| India | 5,334 |
| Spain | 4,848 |
| Norway | 4,711 |

The top non-Danish emigration groups are **EU labour migrants cycling through** (Romania, Poland, Germany, Italy) — a pattern that's been stable for decades. The US figure (12,172 out) is high but US immigration is also high; net US migration remains positive.

---

## 7. Who has the strongest net pull?

Most positive net migration by citizenship, 2023–2025:

| Citizenship | Net |
|-------------|-----|
| Ukraine | +15,810 |
| Nepal | +7,351 |
| Germany | +6,404 |
| Bangladesh | +5,025 |
| Iran | +4,555 |
| Denmark (returnees) | +4,055 |
| Italy | +3,749 |
| India | +3,714 |

The composition is shifting toward South/Southeast Asia and away from EU-internal mobility — which is a demographic story about **where Denmark is successfully competing** for talent and residency, not necessarily a brand story.

## Key soundbites for the podcast

- *"Net migration to Denmark has been essentially flat at 30–32k for three consecutive years. If the brand were cracking, you'd expect to see that number falling — it isn't."*

- *"The 2022-to-2023 'drop' everyone cites was 100% Ukraine normalisation. Comparing anything to 2022 migration numbers is like comparing your average heating bill to the one winter your pipes froze."*

- *"The year-on-year change in international net migration for 2024→2025 was minus 221 people. The standard deviation in this dataset is 15,000. That's not a trend — that's a rounding error."*

- *"The EU trend is the one to watch — that's a genuine multi-year shift. But it's happening across all Northern European destinations simultaneously, which points to structural EU mobility patterns, not Denmark-specific reputation effects."*

- *"Denmark is still receiving roughly two internationals for every 1.3 that leave. The outflow question is a real one to monitor, but we are nowhere near a reversal."*
